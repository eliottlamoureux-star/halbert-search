From:

North of Jewfish Creek

Dear Friend and Subscriber,

      We live in a jam-packed world.

      It reminds me of a guy who, years ago, did a lot of public speaking.  He had a neat gimmick for getting people’s attention.  He had a huge glass jar on stage with him.  He’d tell the audience he was going to fill up the jar.  Then he did.  With rocks.  Rocks about half the size, I think, of a woman’s fist.  Then, he’d tell the audience the jar was full and could they see that?  When people in the audience said yes, they could see that he would say “Hold on a minute.  The jar isn’t really full.”

      Then, he would dump gravel into the jar which would settle in the spaces between the rocks.  He would say that now the jar was full.  When the audience agreed he’d say “No, the jar is not full yet.”

      Then, he’d put sand in the jar.  The sand would settle in spaces between the gravel and the rocks.  He’d get the audience to agree the jar was full and then, he’d tell them otherwise.

      Then, he’d put water in the jar and allow as how, at last, the jar was indeed full.

      Nowadays, I’m guessing, some hot shot would say the jar was not full.  And then he’d insert a hose into the water and pump oxygen into it until the water was saturated with oxygen and only then would he say the jar was full.

      But wait!  Along comes another guy who shoots sub-atomic particles into the jar and saturates it with sub-atomic debris and then say how the jar is full.

      And along comes a guy with an x-ray machine... to hell with it.  I got lost way back there when the guy poured water into the jar.

      But hey, you wanna see some jam-packing going on in your everyday life?  You do?  I can’t imagine why.  But you can see all of this hysterical jam-packing just by turning on your TV.  Watch any show and wait t’il the commercials come on.  You won’t have to wait long.  First, there will be about a dozen of them one right after the other.  No fading to black for a second between commercials- we’re watching one commercial when we’re hit with another with no time to catch our breath.  Then wham!  Another... and another and another, etc.

      And the commercial itself will be superheated and give you no time whatsoever to absorb one image until there’s another.  I think the average 10 second commercial bombards you with about 50 to 60 images.  One cut after another.  1/100 of a second image then cut!  ½ second image cut... 1/3 of a second image cut!  Then image cut!  Image cut, image cut!... Cut!... Cut!... Cut!...

      The sales message?  It doesn’t survive all those cuts.  Your mind will be chopped up with all those cuts and, if someone asks you what you just watched you’ll sit there numb and mutter, “I don’t know really.  I think it was something about a car?

      I’d like to see a car commercial where the only image you saw was that of a new car.  It would stay on screen for the full 10 seconds and there would be a voiceover from a celebrity spokesperson that said, “This is the new Sharpmobile.  It’s really a great car.  You should buy one.”  And this commercial would give maybe a full 6 seconds of relief from the continuing blitz.

      6 seconds!  Wow!  A whole 6 seconds of calm.

      Come to think of it, you know what I didn’t write so far  when I described that blitz of commercials?  I didn’t write about the sound tracks that accompany all of those commercials.  It’s extremely loud, harsh and grating, violent drums, staccato rythmns, screeching horns, explosions.  Yes, your ears have to be tormented along with your visual cortex.

      Now let’s talk about the average Americans’ jam-packed life.  He gets up, turns the radio on so he can hear it while he showers.  After the shower, he fires up his trusty I-Pod, puts on a pair of headphones and listens to hip-hop or some kind of heavy metal music.  Then, it’s off to his computer to check his e-mails.  While the computer plays music through it’s little speakers, then he gets in his car and turns the key to start the car and immediately the car radio is blaring.  He arrives at work, turns off the car and turns on the I-Pod so there will be no dead air as he walks across the parking lot from his car to his office.  He walks in the office and is immediately given a list of phone calls that are waiting for him.  As he walks a short distance to his desk, he can hear seven different radios with seven different stations.  And then he sits down and... never mind.  You can guess what the rest of his day will be like.

      Where I live, there are the sounds of twenty-four hour a day construction work as they endeavor to surround my building with other new buildings.  The cacophony never stops.  I was reminded of all of this as I listened to the CD recordings of my Root Canal Seminar in Los Angeles.  Most of the seminar was taken up by people asking me questions.  They literally sucked me dry.  I am not sure I know anything about marketing that I didn’t reveal at that seminar.  And I revealed a lot of information and inside secrets that I didn’t know I knew.  It was only through the act of teaching to a lot of information starved attendees that I was able to dredge up secrets and techniques I had forgotten.  It was jam-packing in reverse.  Now it was me who bombarded the attendees with information, marketing secrets, sophisticated techniques of marketing I had long forgotten.  There is a moral here.  A lesson.  And that lesson is if you really want to learn something, you need to teach it.  It is the act of teaching that allows your knowledge of a subject to fully blossom.  Your knowledge of a subject will become better organized and you will discover you know a lot more than you thought you knew.  So that’s the lesson of this newsletter.  If you want to more fully understand something, teach what you already know to one or more people, I can sum up this lesson in three words...

 To Learn...TEACH!

      And listen: I haven’t written the detailed sales message that the recordings of my Root Canal Seminar deserves.  In truth, there is so much information on those CD recordings I almost don’t know where to start.  Perhaps I’ll get around to that sometime in the future.  But please take my word for it.  If you want to hear me at my best, while I am being sucked-dry of every marketing secret I know, you need to get these CD’s.  I’m gonna turn it over to Liliana now, who will tell you how to order them.

 
  Sincerely,
 
 

Gary C. Halbert
